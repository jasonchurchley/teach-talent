What does an SEN teaching assistant do?

A clear guide to the SEN teaching assistant role: daily duties, the needs you might support, useful training and how it differs from a general TA role.

The short answer

An SEN teaching assistant supports pupils with special educational needs and disabilities so they can take part in learning and school life. The role can be one-to-one, small group or across a specialist class.

A typical day

Days usually start by preparing resources and checking the plan with the class teacher or SENCO. Through the day you might adapt tasks, use visual supports, help a pupil regulate their emotions, support personal care and record progress against EHCP outcomes.

Needs you might support

Common needs include autism, ADHD, speech, language and communication needs, social, emotional and mental health needs, moderate learning difficulties and physical or sensory needs. Every pupil is different, which is why the right match matters.

Useful training

Safeguarding, autism awareness, Makaton, Team Teach or other positive behaviour support training, and first aid all make you a stronger candidate.
